Heat Transfer

How energy moves — conduction, convection & radiation

What is heat transfer?

Heat is energy on the move. Whenever two places have different temperatures, energy tends to flow from the warmer place toward the cooler one until they even out. Scientists group that flow into three clear modes: conduction (through touching matter), convection (by moving fluids), and radiation (as waves through empty space). Explore each demo below — drag, click, and tweak the sliders to see the physics in action.

Conduction

Energy passed atom-to-atom through a solid — no bulk motion of the material itself.

Imagine a metal rod. Atoms on the hot end vibrate hard and bump their neighbors. Those neighbors bump the next ones, and so on — like a chain of people passing a message. Heat creeps along the solid even though the atoms mostly stay in place. Metals conduct well because their outer electrons move freely and carry energy fast; wood and air conduct poorly (they are good insulators).

Convection

Heat carried by the bulk motion of a fluid — warm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks.

Warm air or water expands, becomes less dense, and floats upward. Cooler fluid is denser and sinks. That creates a looping current called a convection cell. You feel it as a draft above a radiator, see it as soup swirling on the stove, and live inside giant versions of it in weather and ocean currents.

  • Click anywhere in the box to place or move a heat source.
  • Adjust heat strength and fluid viscosity to change the flow.
  • Warm (orange/red) blobs rise; cool (blue) blobs sink and close the loop.

Radiation

Energy traveling as electromagnetic waves — no touching matter required.

Every warm object gives off infrared light (and hotter ones give off visible light too). Those waves can cross a vacuum — that is how sunlight warms Earth across empty space. Dark surfaces absorb radiation well; shiny or white surfaces reflect more of it. A barrier in the path can block or absorb the waves.

  • Drag the source temperature slider — hotter means brighter, faster waves.
  • Toggle the blocker to put a shield in the path of the rays.
  • Notice: waves still travel through the empty (vacuum) region with no particles needed.
